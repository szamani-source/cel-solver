FAQ and known issues

Answers to the questions new users ask first, what the common error messages mean, and the limits of the code as it stands. Each known issue names the design note in docs/decisions/ that records it.

Setting up a case

Mohr-Coulomb or Drucker-Prager?

For bulk solids, start with Mohr-Coulomb: its friction angle is the one quoted in the literature and on data sheets, and it gave the best discharge rate in the Couto silo (123.8 kg/s against the Beverloo range of 110 to 131). Drucker-Prager is smoother and slightly cheaper. If you use it, take the fit plane_strain; the compression fit made the same silo discharge at 45 kg/s instead of 110. See Material models.

Which flow rules should I switch on?

For silo and hopper discharge use the set in Recommended rule sets: shearCap, normalCap, spreadLayers, flowImpedance (with \(\mu(I)\)) and fullBlend. For clay under footings and piles leave them off; those cases use the set of decisions 0055 and 0056. The rules change how partly filled cells and faces at yield behave; they do not change the material law.

Why are the masses in the log so small in 2D?

A 2D (plane-strain) mesh is one cell thick, and every mass and flow rate in the log is for that thickness. Divide by the thickness to get values per metre. Make the thickness equal to the in-plane cell size: the time step is taken from \(V^{1/3}\), so a thin slab of large cells runs with too small a step (Meshing guide).

How do I start from a settled state?

The default is to switch gravity on in a full container and let it settle with light damping (gravityRamp, damping, dampingStart). This gave the best wall pressures in the Couto silo (\(K \approx 0.43\)). restRelax drives the stress toward \(K_0 = 1-\sin\varphi\) Jáky 1944 instead. Filling from the top through a narrow inlet doubles the wall pressures, because the impact locks stress in and plain Mohr-Coulomb has no compaction; use the cap if you need a filling history. See Starting at rest.

How fine must the mesh be?

The cells at an outlet set the discharge rate: on the Couto silo, 25 mm cells gave 21 kg/s and 3 to 5 mm cells 106 to 124 kg/s. Use cells of 3 to 5 mm at outlets, finer cells near walls, and coarser cells higher up. Without the nonlocal law a shear band is one to four cells wide on any mesh, so results that depend on the band (collapse run-out, footing loads) change with the mesh; switch on nonlocalA to give the band a physical width. Details in the Meshing guide.

The run is slow. What sets the time step?

The elastic wave speed \(c = \sqrt{M/\rho}\) and the smallest cell, through maxCo (default 0.4). Halving the smallest cell halves the step; quadrupling \(E\) halves it too. Use graded meshes so that only the cells that need it are small, and run large cases on several processors (Running in parallel).

How do I restart a run?

Set startFrom latestTime in controlDict and run again. The restart is exact only when the run wrote with writeFormat binary or writePrecision 17 (decision 0081); the tutorials write ASCII with 8 digits, which is fine for viewing but restarts a little off. A free or deformable body must restart from a time that celFoam wrote, because its state is stored there.

Known issues

These are the limits found so far, with the decision that records each one. None of them stops a run; they bound how far a result can be trusted.

Silos and hoppers

  • Couto silo at rest and in discharge. Wall pressures at rest (GA0) are about 20 to 25 % above the measurements; in discharge GA1 reads 26 % low and GA2 7 % low. The Abaqus model of Zheng and Yu 2015 is also low at GA2. Wall friction, normalCap and flowImpedance were ruled out as causes.
  • Silo with \(\mu(I)\) (decisions 0045, 0046). The discharge rate depends on how cells that have just loosened are counted, about 20 % between stiffnesses; dilatancy and blended rules did not cure it.
  • Cohesive tank creep (decisions 0054, 0097). A tank held up by a cohesive arch keeps creeping out at under 1 % of the Beverloo rate (4.5 % near the critical width).
  • Free-surface tanks without \(\mu(I)\) (decision 0051). A plain Mohr-Coulomb tank with freeSurface yes settles slowly and its bottom stress reads 17 % high, because noise-level void switches full cells to the partly filled rules. Silo runs avoid it with flowImpedance.

Meshes

  • Mesh dependence (decision 0038). Without the nonlocal law, shear bands are one to four cells wide and collapse run-out grows with refinement. The nonlocal law gives them a width set by the grain size.
  • Turned and sheared cells (decision 0106). On meshes whose lines are turned away from the walls, a layer sliding along a wall lifts off slightly (normal speed 0.033 m/s against a limit of 0.0145). Keep mesh lines along the walls, as in the Meshing guide.
  • STL walls (decision 0051). Where an STL floor meets an STL wall, the corner cell has one oblique wall face and is not exactly in equilibrium; a lake in an all-STL tank moves up to 0.3 m/s at the corners without damping. Body-fitted meshes avoid this.

Bodies and geotechnics

  • Footings on sand read high (decisions 0095, 0096). The load includes a numerical rate effect that grows with \(\rho c v\) of the push: at \(\psi = 30^\circ\) it is +160 % at 0.01 m/s and +55 % at 0.00125 m/s. Treat quasi-static loads on frictional material as upper estimates.
  • Projectile penetration (decisions 0074, 0094). A slug into Tresca clay stops 15 % short of the energy estimate from Prandtl 1921.
  • Deformable bodies much finer than the cells (decision 0073) pack the material beneath them (1.039 \(\rho_0\) for a 20 × 20 footing on 1 cm cells). Keep body elements near the cell size; soft bodies need hourglass 0.3.
  • Free body rotating in contact (decision 0058). A symmetric block dropped with inertia picks up a small spin at impact from a few per cent of asymmetry in the reaction.
  • Taylor bar (decision 0098). The shortening matches Abaqus to 0.3 %; the lip at the impact face is first-order and slightly rounded.

Running

  • Parallel round-off (decision 0080). With bodies, caps or void rules, a parallel run matches serial to round-off until a switch flips, then differs as much as a serial run with a \(10^{-12}\) perturbation would (footing force 2 to 4 %, tank discharge rate unchanged).
  • Restart precision (decision 0081). Exact only with binary output or 17 digits.
  • Explicit cost. Stiff materials and small cells make many steps; the full test suite takes about 3 hours.

Getting help

Open an issue in the repository szamani-source/cel-solver with the case directory (without time folders), the end of log.celFoam and the version (git rev-parse --short HEAD). The status of every work package and the full list of open issues is kept in docs/STATUS.md.
