Theory II: material models

The stress update at one point: elasticity, the yield surfaces of metals, clay and sand, rate-dependent and nonlocal friction for flowing grains, cohesion, compaction, and friction against walls.

One interface for every model

Every model maps a strain increment \(\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon\), the rotated old stress \(\tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma}\) and the history \(\mathbf h_n\) to the new stress and history. The Eulerian material and the deformable bodies call the same code (library celConstitutive). Each model was first written in Python and the C++ version is checked against it point by point to \(10^{-14}\).

All models start with an elastic predictor. With Lamé's constants \(\lambda = \frac{E\nu}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}\) and \(G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}\):

$$\boldsymbol\sigma^{\text{trial}} = \tilde{\boldsymbol\sigma} + \lambda\operatorname{tr}(\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon)\,\mathbf I + 2G\,\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon .$$

If no yield function is positive, \(\boldsymbol\sigma_{n+1} = \boldsymbol\sigma^{\text{trial}}\). Otherwise a return mapping brings the stress back to the yield surface Simo and Hughes 1998 de Souza Neto et al. 2008, and the plastic strain grows by \(\Delta\boldsymbol\varepsilon^p = \mathbb C^{-1}:(\boldsymbol\sigma^{\text{trial}}-\boldsymbol\sigma_{n+1})\). The returns are closed-form (exact for the perfectly plastic and linearly hardening models), so the result does not depend on the size of the step.

Notation: \(s_1 \ge s_2 \ge s_3\) are the principal stresses (tension positive), \(p_m = \tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol\sigma\) the mean stress, \(p = -p_m\) the pressure, \(\mathbf s\) the deviatoric stress and \(J_2 = \tfrac12\mathbf s:\mathbf s\).

Linear elastic

type linearElastic: the predictor alone, isotropic Hooke's law with \(E\) and \(\nu\). Used for rigid-ish deformable bodies and for wave tests.

von Mises (J2) plasticity

type vonMises, for metals and for undrained clay (where it is equivalent to Tresca in plane strain):

$$f = \sqrt{3J_2} - (\sigma_y + H\,\bar\varepsilon^p) \le 0,$$

with yield stress \(\sigma_y\) and linear hardening modulus \(H\). The radial return scales the deviatoric trial stress back to the cylinder; it is exact for linear hardening Simo and Hughes 1998.

Mohr-Coulomb

type mohrCoulomb, the standard model for soils and bulk solids. In principal stresses the yield function and the plastic potential are

$$f = (s_1 - s_3) + (s_1+s_3)\sin\varphi - 2c\cos\varphi,\qquad g = (s_1 - s_3) + (s_1+s_3)\sin\psi ,$$

with friction angle \(\varphi\), cohesion \(c\) and dilation angle \(\psi\). On any plane the shear stress is at most \(c + \sigma_n'\tan\varphi\), with \(\sigma_n'\) the compressive normal stress. The flow is non-associated: \(\psi < \varphi\) gives the moderate dilation real sands show; \(\psi = \varphi\) would greatly overpredict it.

The return is solved exactly in principal space: first to the main plane; if that crosses an edge, to the edge (triaxial compression corner \(s_1 = s_2\) or extension corner \(s_2 = s_3\)); if that fails, to the apex at \(s_1 = s_2 = s_3 = c\cot\varphi\). Each is a 1×1 or 2×2 linear solve de Souza Neto et al. 2008. With \(\varphi = 0\) the model is Tresca, used for undrained clay with \(c = s_u\).

Tension cut-off. sigmaT adds three Rankine planes \(s_i \le \sigma_T\) with \(0 \le \sigma_T \le c\cot\varphi\), and an active-set return over the main, edge and tension planes (decision 0015).

Drucker-Prager

type druckerPrager, a smooth cone Drucker and Prager 1952:

$$f = \sqrt{J_2} + \eta\,p_m - \xi c,\qquad g = \sqrt{J_2} + \bar\eta\,p_m ,$$

matched to Mohr-Coulomb by the option fit de Souza Neto et al. 2008:

fit\(\eta\)\(\xi\)Matches Mohr-Coulomb
compression (default)\(\dfrac{6\sin\varphi}{\sqrt3(3-\sin\varphi)}\)\(\dfrac{6\cos\varphi}{\sqrt3(3-\sin\varphi)}\)in triaxial compression (outer cone)
extension\(\dfrac{6\sin\varphi}{\sqrt3(3+\sin\varphi)}\)\(\dfrac{6\cos\varphi}{\sqrt3(3+\sin\varphi)}\)in triaxial extension (inner cone)
plane_strain\(\dfrac{3\tan\varphi}{\sqrt{9+12\tan^2\varphi}}\)\(\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{9+12\tan^2\varphi}}\)in plane-strain failure

\(\bar\eta\) follows from \(\psi\) by the same formula. The return goes to the cone, or to the apex when the cone return would cross the axis. In silo flow the compression fit is far too strong: use plane_strain, which gave a discharge rate within 12 % of Mohr-Coulomb's for the Couto silo.

Cohesion and tensile strength

A cohesive material (\(c > 0\)) can stand in a vertical cut and arch over an outlet. In the free-surface solver, a contact face may carry tension up to the tensile strength: sigmaT if given, otherwise \(c\cot\varphi\), the apex of the cone. Beyond it the material tears and void opens (decision 0054). A material with \(c = 0\) or \(\sigma_T = 0\) carries no tension at all.

Checks: a cohesive column with a free face stands or fails within Terzaghi's limits for the critical height, \(H_c = (2.67\ \text{to}\ 4)\,\frac{c}{\gamma}\tan(45^\circ+\varphi/2)\) Terzaghi 1943, and a cohesive silo arches over outlets narrower than Jenike's critical width and flows through wider ones Jenike 1964 (see Validation).

Rate-dependent friction \(\mu(I)\)

Dense granular flows are well described by a friction coefficient that depends on the inertial number GDR MiDi 2004 Jop et al. 2006,

$$I = \frac{\dot\gamma\,d}{\sqrt{p/\rho_s}},\qquad \mu(I) = \mu_s + \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_s}{I_0/I + 1},$$

the ratio of the time a grain takes to fall into a hole under the confining pressure \(p\) to the time of the shear \(1/\dot\gamma\); \(d\) is the grain diameter and \(\rho_s\) the grain density. At rest \(\mu = \mu_s = \tan\varphi\); in fast flow it approaches \(\mu_2\). Glass beads give \(\mu_s = \tan 20.9^\circ\), \(\mu_2 = \tan 32.76^\circ\), \(I_0 = 0.279\) Jop et al. 2005.

celFoam puts the law inside the Mohr-Coulomb and Drucker-Prager returns (decision 0037). The friction sets the mobilised angle \(\varphi_m = \arctan\mu\); a base return at \(\varphi_m\) gives the plastic shear increment \(\Delta\gamma = \sqrt{2\,\mathbf e^p:\mathbf e^p}\) and the pressure after the return, hence \(I = (\Delta\gamma/\Delta t)\,d\sqrt{\rho_s/p}\). The friction that agrees with its own \(I\), the root of \(R(\mu) = \mu - \mu(I(\mu))\), is found by the Illinois method to \(10^{-14}\mu_2\).

The \(\mu(I)\) law is ill-posed at small and large \(I\) in time-dependent flows Barker et al. 2015. The optional regularisation of Barker and Gray 2017 (muIAlpha) replaces the law below its well-posed range by \(\mu = \sqrt{\alpha/\ln(A/I)}\). For \(\mu_s = \tan 35^\circ\) the Jop law has no well-posed range, so the branch cannot be built and it stays off in the tutorials; the nonlocal law below is the cure used instead.

Optional dilatancy

With phi0, phiC, bPhi and KDil the dilation angle follows the state, a compressible \(\mu(I)/\Phi(I)\) law Pailha and Pouliquen 2009:

$$\tan\psi = K_\text{dil}\big(\Phi_s - \Phi_\text{eq}(I)\big),\qquad \Phi_\text{eq}(I) = \Phi_C - b_\Phi I,\qquad \Phi_s = \Phi_0\exp(-\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol\varepsilon^p),$$

clipped to \(|\psi| \le \varphi_m\). Dense material dilates while it flows, loose material contracts, and both tend to the critical solid fraction. It did not improve the silo results and is off by default (decision 0048).

Nonlocal friction: shear bands with a physical width

With a local friction law a shear band has no length scale: it narrows to one to four cells on any mesh, and run-outs and band widths change with the mesh (decision 0038). The nonlocal granular fluidity model Kamrin and Koval 2012 Henann and Kamrin 2013 adds a length proportional to the grain size. The plastic shear rate is written as a fluidity \(g\) times the stress ratio,

$$\dot\gamma^p = g\,\mu,\qquad \mu = \frac{\tau}{p},\quad \tau = \sqrt{J_2},$$

and \(g\) obeys a screened Poisson equation, solved on the mesh every step:

$$\nabla^2 g = \frac{1}{\xi^2}\,(g - g_\text{loc}),\qquad \xi = \frac{A\,d}{\sqrt{|\mu - \mu_s|}},\qquad g_\text{loc} = \begin{cases}\sqrt{\dfrac{p}{\rho_s d^2}}\ \dfrac{\mu - \mu_s}{b\,\mu} & \mu > \mu_s\\[1ex] 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$

with \(b = (\mu_2 - \mu_s)/I_0\), the small-\(I\) slope of the \(\mu(I)\) law, and the nonlocal amplitude \(A\) (nonlocalA; 0.48 for glass beads). In uniform shear \(g = g_\text{loc}\) and the local \(\mu(I)\) law is recovered; near a band the fluidity spreads over the cooperativity length \(\xi\), a few grain diameters, so the band has a physical width and flow can creep below the yield stress next to a flowing zone, as in experiments.

In the point update the deviator is scaled down at fixed pressure so that \(\tau = \tau^\text{trial} - G\,\Delta t\,g\,\mu\), with the local part of \(g\) implicit (an explicit \(g\) is unstable) and the nonlocal part from the field equation. A sheared layer on three meshes (cells of 2, 1 and 0.5 \(d\)) gives a band about \(3d\) wide whose width converges, and a velocity profile within 5 % of the 1D nonlocal solution (decision 0111). Cells must be about \(d\) or finer near the band for the converged width; coarser cells still widen the band but do not converge.

With cohesion the law reads the shifted pressure \(p^* = p + c\cot\varphi\) everywhere it reads \(p\), so \(\mu^* = \tau/p^*\) and flow starts at the cohesive yield \(\tau = c + \mu_s p\) (decision 0112).

Compaction cap

A frictional cone alone has no volumetric yield: a deposit loaded by its own weight does not compact. The cap (capLambda, decision 0113) adds a volumetric yield \(p \le p_c\) whose preconsolidation pressure \(p_c\) hardens so that loading along the cap follows the normal compression line in its natural-strain form Butterfield 1979 Wood 1990:

$$\varepsilon_v = \lambda^*\ln\frac{p}{p_0},\qquad \lambda^* = \frac{\lambda}{1+e_0},$$

with \(\varepsilon_v\) the compressive volumetric strain. Unloading and reloading below \(p_c\) are elastic with the bulk modulus \(K\) from \(E\) and \(\nu\). After the frictional update, if \(p' > p_c\) the step's volumetric strain is shared so that the new stress lies on the line:

$$p_{c,\text{new}} = p_c\,\exp\!\Big(\frac{p' - p_c}{K\lambda^*}\Big),$$

and the stress is scaled by \(p_{c,\text{new}}/p'\) towards the apex, so it stays inside the cone. The cap needs \(K\lambda^* > p\) (the elastic line stiffer than the compression line). \(p_c\) starts at \(\max(p, p_0)\): material is normally consolidated where it is first loaded. A settling column follows \(\ln(\rho/\rho_0) = p_0/K + \lambda^*\ln(p/p_0)\) within 2 % in every loaded cell.

At-rest stress and relaxation

A deposit settled under gravity in a wide container carries a horizontal stress \(K_0\) times the vertical one, with \(K_0 \approx 1 - \sin\varphi\) for normally consolidated sand Jaky 1944. Grains that land at speed when a silo is filled from the top lock in a much larger sideways stress (\(K \approx 0.87\) against 0.42), which a perfectly plastic model inside its cone never releases. The option restRelax relaxes the horizontal stress of full, slow cells towards \(K_0\) times the vertical stress with a time constant \(T\) (decision 0114):

$$\dot\sigma_h = -\frac{1}{T}\big(\sigma_h - K_0\,\sigma_v\big)\qquad\text{where } |\mathbf v| < v_\text{rest}.$$

Friction against walls

Walls, outlets while closed, and STL bodies use Coulomb friction with a wall friction angle \(\delta_w\) and, on bodies, an adhesion \(a\): the shear traction is at most \(a + \tan\delta_w\) times the wall pressure, and the material separates from the wall rather than pull on it (Theory I). The wall friction coefficient \(\mu_w = \tan\delta_w\) is measured with a shear box against the wall material; the Couto wheat on steel has \(\mu_w = 0.20\) Couto et al. 2013. It controls how much of the weight in a silo the walls carry (Janssen's effect, see Validation).
