Theory III: bodies and contact

The Lagrangian side of celFoam: rigid bodies given by STL surfaces, deformable bodies of hex8 elements, and how both exchange forces with the Eulerian material through the cells their surfaces cut.

Overview

A body is a closed surface that moves through the fixed Eulerian mesh. Where it lies, cells are cut into an open part, which holds material, and a covered part, which does not. The body surface inside each cut cell becomes one more face of that cell, solved like a wall with the body's velocity. The force on the body is minus the sum of those face forces. This is a cut-cell (immersed boundary) coupling Mittal and Iaccarino 2005 rather than the penalty contact of most CEL codes: it needs no penalty stiffness, and no material ever crosses the body surface (decisions 0042, 0047). The same machinery gives fixed walls of any shape: a body that does not move.

Exact cut geometry

For every cell near a body celFoam computes, without sampling (decision 0043):

  • the covered length of each mesh edge, from its crossings with the surface triangles;
  • the covered area of each face by Green's theorem over its covered part;
  • the covered volume of each cell by the divergence theorem over the clipped triangles and the covered parts of its faces.

Material then fills only the open volume \(V(1-\alpha_\text{body})\) and crosses only the open part of each face. The wall face of a cut cell has the area vector that closes the cell, \(\mathbf S_w = -\sum_f \mathbf S_f^\text{open}\), so a uniform stress gives exactly no force. Its Riemann solution is the wall solution of Theory I (no tension, Coulomb friction with the body's friction angle and adhesion) in the body's moving frame.

Cut cells with an open fraction below one half are mixed with a full neighbour, so their small volume does not limit the time step; covered cells next to material hold the average state of their open neighbours as ghost values, so reconstruction and remap need no special case near a body.

Rigid bodies

A rigid body carries a reference point \(\mathbf c\) (its centre) and a rotation \(\mathbf R\) from its pose at time zero, so a surface point \(\mathbf X_0\) is at

$$\mathbf x(t) = \mathbf c(t) + \mathbf R(t)\,(\mathbf X_0 - \mathbf c_0),\qquad \dot{\mathbf x} = \mathbf v(t) + \boldsymbol\omega(t)\times(\mathbf x - \mathbf c(t)).$$

Prescribed motion. \(\mathbf v(t)\) and \(\boldsymbol\omega(t)\) are given as functions of time. Over a step, \(\mathbf c\) moves by the exact integral of \(\mathbf v\), and \(\mathbf R\) turns by the angle \(|\mathbf W|\) about \(\mathbf W/|\mathbf W|\), \(\mathbf W = \int\boldsymbol\omega\,dt\), which is exact while the axis is fixed. The force of the material on the body is reported, not applied.

Free bodies (decision 0058). With a mass \(m\) and inertia \(\mathbf J_0\) the motion follows from the force \(\mathbf F\) and moment \(\mathbf M\) of the material, and gravity, by central differences with staggered velocities, like the material:

$$\mathbf v \mathrel{+}= \Delta t\Big(\frac{\mathbf F}{m} + \mathbf g\Big),\qquad \boldsymbol\omega \mathrel{+}= \Delta t\,\mathbf J^{-1}\big(\mathbf M - \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf J\boldsymbol\omega\big),\qquad \mathbf J = \mathbf R\mathbf J_0\mathbf R^{\mathsf T}.$$

A body dropped on a deposit comes to rest carrying its own weight to 0.01 %, with momentum conserved to \(10^{-5}\).

Moving cut cells

When a body moves, each step runs (decision 0053):

  1. the Lagrange step at the old pose, with the body's velocity on its wall faces;
  2. the bodies move;
  3. the remap, in which each wall cell is mapped onto the open volume the body leaves, \(V_t = V_o + \Delta t\,\mathbf v_w\cdot\mathbf A\), so a full cell stays full and no material is squeezed into the part the body takes;
  4. the cut cells are recomputed at the new pose and the material carried onto them: a cell the body now covers hands its mass, momentum, stress and history to a neighbour; a cell the body uncovers starts empty.

Uncovered cells start as void, so moving and free bodies need freeSurface yes.

Deformable bodies: hex8 elements

A deformable body is a box of 8-node hexahedra integrated explicitly, the standard element of explicit codes (decision 0070). The gradient operator is the mean of the shape-function gradients over the element Flanagan and Belytschko 1981,

$$\mathbf B_I = \frac{1}{V}\int_V \nabla N_I\,dV = \frac{1}{V}\sum_\text{faces}\int N_I\,\mathbf n\,dA, \qquad \mathbf f_I^\text{int} = V\,\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf B_I ,$$

with each face integral exact (2×2 Gauss points on the bilinear face), so \(\sum_I \mathbf x_I\otimes\mathbf B_I = \mathbf I\) and the element passes the patch test for any shape. Masses are lumped; velocities are staggered in time (central difference); the strain increment and spin come from the velocity gradient on the mid-step shape; the stress is rotated with Hughes-Winget and updated by the same material models as the Eulerian side.

Hourglass control

One-point integration leaves zero-energy (hourglass) modes. They are resisted with the Flanagan-Belytschko base vectors \(\boldsymbol\gamma_a\), orthogonal to every linear velocity field Flanagan and Belytschko 1981:

  • stiffness (default; LS-DYNA type 4): hourglass forces grow with the integrated hourglass velocity, \(\mathbf Q_a \mathrel{+}= \Delta t\,k\,\mathbf q_a\), \(\mathbf q_a = \sum_I \mathbf v_I\gamma_{aI}\), \(\mathbf f_I = -\sum_a \mathbf Q_a\gamma_{aI}\), with \(k = \kappa\,M V\sum_I|\mathbf B_I|^2/8\) (\(\kappa\) = hourglass, \(M\) the P-wave modulus). Conserves energy; for elastic bodies.
  • viscous (LS-DYNA type 1): forces from the current hourglass velocity with coefficient \(\kappa\,\rho c V^{2/3}/4\). For bodies in large plastic flow such as impact and penetration (decision 0071).

Coupling a deformable body

The body's surface is the boundary of its elements, two triangles per element face, and is cut into the mesh like an STL. The force of the material on each wall cell goes to the nodes of the nearest surface triangle with barycentric weights. The elements run one coupling step ahead and are subcycled with their own stable step, so the surface moves exactly as the material saw it move (decision 0072). An optional viscous damping \(2\zeta\rho c L_e\mathbf D\) stops a stiff body from ringing after an impact.

The element's stable step is \(V/(\text{largest face area})/c\). Keep elements close to the Eulerian cell size: much finer bodies over-pack the material beneath them (decision 0073).

Bodies in parallel runs

Every processor holds the whole surface of every body (walls and inserts are small); each processor cuts its own cells. Body forces and moments are summed over processors. Small cut cells are not mixed across processor boundaries; if one would need it, the log reports it as unmixed (decision 0080).
